# Design: Day-14 template verdict

Generated by /office-hours on 2026-10-29
Branch: deploy
Repo: nomideusz/dispatcher
Status: APPROVED

## Problem Statement

The catalog's monthly kickback ($184 accrued in the last 30 days, $382 lifetime) depends on which templates get published, but whether a template pays takes about 90 days to show. Publishing runs at ~2.7 templates a day (80 of 151 in the last 30 days), so about 240 more templates ship before any month's batch gets its verdict. The business learns too slowly to steer its own publishing.

## Demand Evidence

Live numbers from Dispatcher (`dispatcherctl`, 2026-10-29):

- 151 published templates; 1,593 projects ever made from them by other users; 1,178 live.
- 32 of 151 have ever paid. Twenty CRM is 49% of lifetime kickback ($187 from 5 live projects); the top 6 are 83%.
- Pay rate rises with age, so "never paid" mostly measures youth, not bad picks. Accrued 30-day earnings (`earningsLast30Days`) show the same shape, so payout lag doesn't explain it:

  | Age | Templates | Ever paid | Lifetime $ |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | under 30 days | 80 | 2 (2%) | $7 |
  | 30–90 days | 53 | 18 (34%) | $53 |
  | 90–180 days | 18 | 12 (67%) | $322 |

- Money follows spend per live project, not project counts:

  | Services | Templates | Live projects | Earned 30d | Per live project |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 1 | 59 | 1,087 | $42 | $0.04 |
  | 2–3 | 52 | 45 | $9 | $0.21 |
  | 4+ | 40 | 46 | $133 | $2.88 |

## Status Quo

- Picking has no single rule. Asked how the next template gets chosen, the answer was "1, 2, and 4 - all are important" (volume, demand data, originals/gaps).
- In practice the rule is volume plus keyword-claiming: 9 templates shipped on 2026-10-28, several named `Keyword | App` (e.g. `Analytics | Rybbit`, `Helpdesk | Frappe`). None came off Dispatcher's own Candidates shortlist (Nextcloud, Payload CMS, Discourse, Docmost, Sevo Panel).
- The keyword experiment's only full result: `CRM | EspoCRM` has ranked #1 on Railway search for "crm" since 2026-09-27 and got 1 project and $0.86 in 32 days.
- Many of the owners' own proposals are originals (apps never published on Railway) that the agent advised against each time. With Twenty excluded, those picks hold up against the agent's picks on kickback. That is the owners' read; it isn't measured in Dispatcher yet.
- Health has been the main maintenance effort. When Twenty CRM's health dropped, it fell in the results for "Twenty" and "Twenty CRM" and new deploys stopped. Today it ranks #13 for "twenty", #8 for "twenty crm" and #9 for "twentycrm".
- Nobody knows where deployers come from. Railway gives creators no traffic-source data, only aggregate deployments, earnings and support health.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

- **User:** the two catalog owners, deciding each day what to publish next and which templates to keep maintaining. Agents read the same answer through `dispatcherctl`.
- **Wedge:** one state per template, set 14 days after it's published: keep, watch or no signal (plus the placeholders too early and pre-tracking). It ships as a column on Templates and the `GET /api/analytics/verdicts` endpoint (agents read it with `dispatcherctl get`), before any new pages.

## Constraints

- **Snapshots began on 2026-08-26, but `templateMetrics` (accrued earnings) only on 2026-09-10.** On older rows, `total_earnings` is a copy of `total_payout` (the backfill in `openDB`), not accrual. So the usable cohort is templates with a real `templateMetrics` row in their day-14 window (eng review R1; about the ones published on or after 2026-08-28). Today 22 of them have reached day 45. The 46 published from 2026-08-28 to 2026-09-30 reach day 90 between 2026-11-25 and 2026-12-29.
- `services` is 0 on snapshots taken before it was recorded, so a template's service count is read from its first snapshot at or after day 14 with `services > 0`.
- Railway exposes no deploy-source data, so channel questions can only be tested indirectly (before/after comparisons, external links).
- The data needed is already stored: `template_snapshots` carries `total_earnings`, `earnings_last30_days`, `active_projects`, `services` and `sampled_at` hourly (`db.go`).
- Single Go binary with DuckDB and an embedded SPA. No new services or dependencies.

## Premises

1. The bottleneck is learning speed, not picking skill. 67% of templates 90+ days old have paid, but about 240 more ship before a batch's verdict lands.
2. *(Revised twice: after the second opinion, then at approval.)* Search position matters, for the queries people actually type, and health drives position.
   - The Search page's position curve gives the share of a keyword's 90-day projects by position: #1 38%, #2 18%, #3 13%, #4 9%. The page itself calls this an upper bound, since popular templates may rank high because they're popular.
   - Twenty CRM is the natural experiment that removes that doubt: its health dropped, it ranked lower for its own name, and new deploys stopped.
   - Generic keywords are weaker than their demand figure suggests. "crm" shows 388 projects in 90 days, but #3 and #4 for "crm" (113 and 115 projects) are Twenty templates people find by name, while #1 EspoCRM got 1.
6. *(Added at approval.)* Originals are worth publishing on their own terms.
   - Publishing first claims the clean slug, which may matter long-term.
   - Unique, useful templates make the marketplace better and promote interesting projects. That matters to the owners beyond kickback, while 25 copies of the same app is a race for easy money.
   - So the verdict judges how a template is doing. It never decides whether an original gets built.
3. Agents are a plausible, growing channel (Railway MCP `search_templates`/`deploy_template`, `.md` deploy manifests) but unproven in this data. Measure before assuming.
4. Money follows usage spend, not deploys. Kickback is 15% of deployer spend (+10% with healthy support), so judge templates by spend per live project.
5. Dispatcher's next job is a faster verdict: a signal within ~14 days that predicts whether a template will pay.

## Cross-Model Perspective

Codex was unavailable: its configured model `gpt-5.5` isn't supported on the ChatGPT-account login. A fresh Claude subagent did the cold read instead (same model family, so its agreement carries less weight than an outside model's would).

- **Steelman:** a portfolio fund for Railway templates with Dispatcher as the risk desk. The edge is a short feedback loop: kill or double down within weeks, then point publishing volume at the proven bets.
- **Key insight:** the "all are important" answer is a picking process with no decision rule. The fix is a gate where one number overrules taste, and that number is expected spend per live project.
- **Challenged premise #2** (accepted): people do search, they search by brand. `Keyword | App` names can bury the brand for the people who type it.
- **Prototype:** backtest day-7/14 accrued earnings and spend per live project against later payout from data already in DuckDB, then add a Verdict column and one CLI command. Skip source attribution, ML and new pages.
- **Where I disagreed:** its "no new template ships while N await a verdict" rule is a business-policy call for the owners, so the design shows the backlog without blocking on it.
- **Where the owner disagreed:** "point publishing volume at the proven bets" ignores originals, which have value beyond kickback (premise 6). The owner also rejected the first revision of premise 2, with the Search page's position data and Twenty's health history as evidence.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Day-14 verdict (chosen)
Backtest an early signal from existing snapshots, then a Verdict column (keep / watch / no signal) on Templates, read by agents through `dispatcherctl get /api/analytics/verdicts`. Effort S (human ~3 days / CC ~1–2 hours).

### Approach B: Cohort ledger
Per-template trajectories (days 7/14/30/90), cohorts by kind, size and naming, a Publishing view with the verdict backlog, and a keyword-vs-brand naming experiment. Ruled out for now: it builds views before knowing whether day 14 predicts anything. Upgrade path once A's signal holds.

### Approach C: Rank Candidates by spend
Score the market by expected spend per live project instead of 90-day deploys. Ruled out for now: it does nothing for the 80 templates already shipped, its proxy (service count) isn't backtested yet, and ranking by existing deploys can only point at apps that are already on Railway, never at originals (premise 6).

## Recommended Approach

**A: Day-14 verdict.** It turns premise 5 into a tested signal from data already in DuckDB, and both B and C depend on that signal.

1. **Cohort and real readings** *(eng review R1, R2)*. Every read uses only snapshots with `total_deployments IS NOT NULL`: the rows where `templateMetrics` actually came back. The boot backfill (`db.go:141`) copies payout into `total_earnings` on every other row, including after 2026-09-10, so a date cutoff can't separate them. Day 0 is a template's first `status = 'PUBLISHED'` snapshot (the `seen` CTE in `timeline.go`), not `publishedAt`. A template first seen at the very first sample (`trackedSince`) was already live before tracking, so it is `pre-tracking` and never backtested (R6). A template with no real row in its day-14 window has no reading and is never backtested.
2. **Predictors, read at the real snapshot closest to day 14, inside day 13–15** *(eng review R3)* (a Go function with a test):
   - **P1, accruing:** absolute `total_earnings` > 0. It is lifetime since publish, so it needs no baseline, and earnings from before the first snapshot are included.
   - **P2, heavy and used:** `active_projects` > 0 on a template with 2+ services (service count from the first snapshot at or after day 14 with `services > 0`, so it stays frozen like P1). Tested on its own, because the service-count proxy hasn't been validated.
3. **Outcome, in a window that doesn't overlap the predictors:** `total_earnings` rises by at least $1 (R8) between the day-14 reading and the day-45 reading (closest real row inside day 44–46; none → left out of the backtest). The horizon moves to day 90 automatically once 20 or more cohort templates have a day-90 snapshot. The payout ledger isn't used.
4. **Pass rule, fixed before running.** The backtest counts only with n ≥ 20 and 4 or more positive outcomes. Each predictor also needs at least 5 templates where it's true and 5 where it's false. A predictor that misses its group minimum, including one whose false group is empty, is "not enough data" for that predictor. A predictor passes if, among templates where it's true, the share whose earnings rise is (a) at least 50% and (b) at least twice the share where it's false.
5. **Two gates: build once, then check at runtime.**
   - **Build gate (one-off).** The backtest function ships first, alone, behind `GET /api/analytics/verdicts` (read with `dispatcherctl get /api/analytics/verdicts`), which returns n, positives, both groups' counts and rates, and pass / fail / not enough data per predictor. The Verdict column gets built only if at least one predictor passes this first run. If every predictor fails, stop: record the output in this doc and build nothing else. "Not enough data" defers instead of ending: the endpoint stays, and any later run where a predictor passes (e.g. once the day-90 cohort lands) reopens the build gate. The endpoint returns the backtest summary before the per-template state exists.
   - **Checkpoints (R7).** Pass/fail is judged once per horizon (day 45, then day 90), the first time that horizon's backtest is decisive: at least one predictor gets a pass or a fail. Each result is stored. While every predictor is still "not enough data", the horizon stays unjudged and waits for its cohort to grow (amended 2026-10-30: a result stored at the first n ≥ 20, and later at the first 4 positives, would have locked day 45 before P1 reached 5 true, which the prod data puts around Nov 30). A predictor still short of its group minimum when the other one decides the horizon stays "not enough data" at that horizon.
   - **Runtime gate (after the column ships).** The same function runs on each request against the stored checkpoint. A state that depends on a predictor (`keep` on P1, `watch` on P2) is shown only while that predictor passes at the latest checkpoint. When none passes, for example after the day-45 to day-90 switch, every cohort template shows `no signal`. The column header hint and the endpoint then say why, e.g. "P1 failed at day 90 (n = 41)", so the change is visible rather than silent. The endpoint always returns each template's frozen P1/P2 values, whatever its state.
6. **Verdict lifecycle.** Each template's day-14 predictor values (P1, P2) are computed from its day-14 snapshot and never change. The displayed state is derived from those frozen values plus the current pass results from step 5. So a template's state changes only when a predictor's pass status changes, never because its own later data changed. The existing columns (Earned 30d, Active, Lifetime) keep showing the current state. Every template gets exactly one state, first match wins:
   1. `too early`: younger than 15 days, so the day 13–15 window has closed and the reading can't move (eng review R5; checked first, outside-voice correction).
   2. `pre-tracking`: no real snapshot in its day-14 window (R1, R3).
   3. `keep`: P1 true, while P1 currently passes.
   4. `watch`: P2 true, while P2 currently passes.
   5. `no signal`: everything else.

   There is no `retire` state. Pay rates still climb from 34% to 67% between day 30–90 and day 90–180, so retiring at day 45 could kill templates that would have paid. Retire is reconsidered once the day-90 horizon is live.
7. **Originals vs. competitive picks.** The backtest reports its rates split by origin: an original (nothing like it on Railway when published) or a competitive pick. Twenty CRM is excluded from that split. Origin is a field the owners set, like `kind`. This tests the owners' read that their originals hold up against the agent's picks.
8. **Surface it.** The backtest runs in a new `verdict.go` handler behind `GET /api/analytics/verdicts` on each request *(eng review D3; `timeline.go` untouched)* (about 150 templates of snapshots, cheap) and returns each template's state plus the backtest's horizon, n, positives, and per-predictor group counts, rates and pass status. All of these are computed, not hard-coded. The Templates table gets a sortable Verdict column (`useSort`, ordered keep, watch, no signal, too early, pre-tracking), and its header hint shows the live n and horizon. Agents read the same fields with `dispatcherctl get /api/analytics/verdicts` (eng review D2: no new CLI commands).

## Open Questions

- Does day-14 accrual actually separate payers? With n = 22 and positives under the new outcome unknown until the backtest runs, the first answer may well be "not enough data".
- Is `total_earnings` accrual reliable at day 14, or does Railway's `templateMetrics` lag by days for new templates?
- Do `Keyword | App` names cost position on the app's own name? Search tracking started on 2026-10-28, so the 9 templates shipped that day give a baseline. Compare their rank for their brand query with how the same apps' other templates rank.
- Should a `keep` template whose health drops get flagged? Twenty shows health falling, then position, then deploys. That's maintenance, not the day-14 verdict, so it's out of scope for A.
- Do agent deploys show up in any measurable way (project names, deploy timing patterns)? Not in scope for A.
- Should the 1-service templates (92% of live projects, $0.04 each) keep getting maintenance at all?

## Success Criteria

- The backtest runs and reports its separation and n. A negative result counts as success: it means a week of building pages was avoided.
- If a predictor passes: every template shows exactly one of the five states, cohort templates 14+ days old have their frozen P1/P2 values in the endpoint, and the Templates column shows the same states.
- Within 30 days of shipping, at least one publishing or maintenance-priority decision is made off a `keep` or `watch` verdict instead of gut feel.

## Dependencies

- Existing: `template_snapshots`, the `seen` CTE in `timeline.go` (copied, not shared), `templates-table.tsx`, `useSort` in `ui/table.tsx`, `dispatcherctl get`.
- No new data collection. One owner-set field, `origin` (original / competitive), in its own `template_origins` table, set with `PUT /api/templates/{id}/origin` from an inline select on Templates (R4). No CLI command (D2).

## The Assignment

Before the backtest ships, mark every published template's origin: an original (nothing like it was on Railway when you published it) or a competitive pick. Note which ones the agent advised against. It's one pass over the Templates list, and it turns "my picks don't look bad" into a number Dispatcher can show, Twenty excluded. If the originals hold up, that's the case for building more of them, with data behind it.

## What I noticed about how you think

- You care how numbers are named. The session started with you catching that "Deployed" on the Money chart looked one-to-one with Published, and that "Active" meant two different things. That instinct, refusing an ambiguous count, is what this design runs on.
- You agreed with every recommendation until the approval step. Then you overturned a premise with your own data (the Search page's position curve, Twenty's health history) and named a value the kickback numbers can't see: marketplace quality. That pushback was the most useful input of the session. It changed premise 2, added premise 6 and replaced the assignment.
- You ship. Nine templates in one day, plus a `Keyword | App` naming experiment run live on the marketplace, is a builder testing ideas in production rather than in a doc. The design keeps that pace and adds a way to read the results sooner.
